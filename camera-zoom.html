Camera Movement and Zoom

Defender X · Preliminary approach · Draft for discussion, nothing built · 23 Oct 2026 · Code as of main c659227

How it works today What assumes a locked camera Decisions First steps Open questions

Later we want a virtual camera that can move (no longer locked on the player) and zoom. Moving the camera changes the world-to-screen transform. Zooming changes macro-pixel rendering too, because something has to give: either the cells grow and shrink, or objects are drawn with more or fewer cells.

This doc covers how rendering works today, what assumes a fixed, player-locked camera, and the decisions to make early, before code starts depending on one answer.

How it works today

World to screen: util/ViewTransform

ViewTransform replaced the old CoordinateTransformer. It's built once per frame from the camera position, the world angle and a screen anchor, and it does three things in order:

  1. translates the point relative to the camera,
  2. rotates it by the world angle,
  3. adds the screen anchor.

It has no scale. One world unit is one virtual-canvas pixel (1920×1080, through a FitViewport). ViewTransform.forPlayer puts the camera on the player (World.cameraX/Y is ship.physics.x/y), takes the world angle from the player's angle, and puts the anchor at the play-area center plus PLAYER_SCREEN_OFFSET (−600, 0). The camera shake is shifted(): the anchor moved by whole cells.

Macro-pixels

  • MACRO_PIXEL_SIZE (4.5) is one cell, in virtual pixels.
  • MacroPixelRenderer draws a sprite by inverse mapping. It walks the screen-aligned cells under the sprite's rotated bounds, snapped to a grid anchored at the canvas origin. Each cell takes the sprite cell under its center (nearest sampling). In other words, the screen grid is fixed and sprites are resampled onto it.
  • Ship sprites are imported from 1024² art at a set length in cells (PLAYER/ENEMY_SPRITE_LENGTH_PX = 40) by SpriteImport. Rocks are generated placeholders.
  • Several things are screen-sized cell grids, all with the same fixed cell size and anchored at the canvas origin:
    • VaporGrid (vapor trails, plus a second one for smoke)
    • CellBackground (stars and nebula)
    • GlowLayer (the glow and shimmer masks)
    • ScreenEffects.CellGrid.gameplay() (cell bloom and the cell grade)
    • ShimmerEffect's u_cell
  • Effects, muzzle flash, engine flame, RCS jets and bullet streaks are measured in cells (dozens of *_CELLS settings) and snapped to the grid (snapToCell).

The cinematics already zoom

ClipRenderer and DocCamera zoom, pan and roll footage over a fixed 9 px cell grid. Each cell samples the frame pixel under its center, the same rule as MacroPixelRenderer. Zoom is clamped to 1.0–2.0. At 1.0, one footage pixel fills one cell. Above it, footage pixels become blocks of cells. Below it would lose detail, which is why the rig can't zoom out past 1:1 (issue #17). This is the precedent closest to the gameplay question.

What assumes the camera is locked on the player and doesn't zoom

The player is assumed to sit at the screen anchor

  • WorldRenderer.drawHulls draws the player at view.screenCenterX/Y, not through worldToScreen.
  • drawShimmerLines puts the player's hull cells at the anchor too.
  • placeBackground centers the stars and nebula on the anchor.
  • OffScreenIndicatorRenderer measures range from view.cameraX/Y (today the same as the player). It also aims the indicator from the anchor to the enemy, so with a moved camera the arrows would point from the wrong place.
  • The view's rotation pivot is the camera, which today is the player.

The view only rotates, it doesn't scale

  • directionToScreen and the "unit length: no zoom" comments in drawEngineFlame, drawJet and EffectRenderer (muzzle) use a world direction as a screen direction of the same length.
  • SpeckField.render repeats the transform's math inline, so it would skip any scale added to ViewTransform.
  • VaporGrid.begin and EffectRenderer cull by screen size in world units. That's only equal while 1 unit = 1 px.

MACRO_PIXEL_SIZE is used as a world length

This is the biggest coupling. The same constant is the screen cell size and the world size of a sprite cell:

  • Pose.cell: where every hull cell is in the world, for hits, fires and hull pieces
  • hull and bounding radii in CollisionSystem, ProjectileSystem, Obstacle, WorldSnapshot (AI sensing) and World (near misses)
  • Effects.ignite (the one-cell fire spacing), VaporTrail (puff spacing, flame tip) and PhysicsState

If zoom changes the screen cell, none of these may change with it, or gameplay would change with the zoom.

Gameplay reads the view

World.processProjectileHits builds its own ViewTransform.forPlayer for the cinematic rule "hits happen where the player sees them". Ships can only be hit inside SHIP_HIT_ZONE_INSET (150 px) of the play area, and rocks anywhere on screen. Near misses and shimmers are on-screen only as well. If this used the real camera, zooming out would make far-off ships hittable, and moving the camera would change who can be hit.

Screen grids are made for one size

VaporGrid and CellBackground reallocate (new arrays, a new Pixmap or Texture, or a new FrameBuffer) whenever the cell size changes. With cells that change size during a smooth zoom, that's a reallocation every frame.

Decisions to make early

D1

What does zoom do to macro-pixels?

Leaning B · needs a look test

A. Cells scale with zoom. The screen cell is MACRO_PIXEL_SIZE × zoom, and every object keeps its cell count. It looks like zooming into a finished 16-bit screen.

Pros:

  • The art, effects and their *_CELLS settings stay as they are.
  • Simple to reason about. One way to build it: render the world at a fixed cell size into a buffer covering 1/zoom of the screen, then scale it up with hard edges, as ClipRenderer does.

Cons:

  • Cells come out uneven at non-integer zoom (4.5 px is already uneven on a 1080p display).
  • Smooth zoom makes the grid "breathe".
  • Zooming out shrinks cells below the chunky look. At 0.5 a cell is 2.25 px, so the 16-bit feel fades exactly when there's the most on screen.
  • The HUD's cell art (indicators, G-LIMIT) would no longer match the world's cells.
  • Every screen grid has to resize.

B. Cells stay fixed; objects get more or fewer cells. The screen grid never changes, and zoom changes how many cells an object covers. This is what the renderer already does for rotation, what the cinematics do, and what SNES Mode 7 does. There are two ways to fill the extra cells:

  • B1, sample. Pass a scale into MacroPixelRenderer and keep nearest sampling. Zoomed in, one art cell becomes a block of 2–3 screen cells, with the same stair-stepping as rotation. Zoomed out, art cells get skipped and thin details flicker.
  • B2, re-import at the on-screen size. The ship source art is 1024², so SpriteImport can make a sprite at lengthPx × zoom cells, cached in steps (for example every 0.25 of zoom), giving real extra detail.
    • Collisions keep using the 1× sprite, so physics doesn't depend on zoom.
    • Damage marks, hull fires and broken-up hulls are kept per sprite cell today. They would need mapping from 1× cells to the detail sprite's cells.
    • The art can pop when it switches between steps.
    • Jesse may want hand-touched art at some steps.
    • Rocks need real art or a generator that works at any size.

Under B, every *_CELLS setting has to say whether it's a world size (grows with zoom: hull fires, debris, a hit's flash on the ship) or a screen size (stays put: streak width, perhaps sparks). That's an audit of roughly 50 settings.

Leaning B. It's the rule the game and the cinematics already use (cells square and uniform to the screen, content resampled under them), and it keeps the look at every zoom. B1 is a cheap first step, and B2 can come later where detail matters. A look test should decide, the way the cinematics approach was decided.

D2

What is zoom for, and how far does it go?

Question for Jesse

The answer sets the range and how much D1 matters. Possible uses:

  • cinematic in-engine moments (launch tube, carrier landing, the jump)
  • zooming out for situational awareness in a big dogfight
  • a punch-in on a kill
  • the debug view

Is zoom continuous or in fixed steps? Does the player control it, or only scripts? Roughly what range? 0.5–2× behaves very differently from 0.25–4×.

D3

When the camera moves, does the ship stay the center of rotation?

Question for Jesse

The pillar is "ship fixed on screen, world rotates around the player". Moving the camera breaks the first half on purpose. Options:

  • (a) The world still turns with the player's angle, pivoting on the player, while the camera only translates.
  • (b) The camera has its own angle, so a scripted shot can hold the carrier level while the player turns.
  • (c) Both, blended, so scripted shots can hand the angle back to the player smoothly.

This decides whether ViewTransform stays "camera + angle + anchor" or becomes a full camera with its own pose. It also bears on scene player step 6, where DocCamera and CameraRig could become the in-game camera too.

D4

Is gameplay tied to the render camera or to a fixed gameplay frame?

Leaning gameplay frame

The hit zone, near misses and anything "on screen" today mean "visible in the player-locked 1× view". Options:

  • Gameplay frame. Gameplay keeps its own fixed view around the player (today's forPlayer), and the render camera is free.
    • Moves and zooms can never change the rules.
    • Zoom out, and you'd see bullets pass through ships in the outer ring.
    • Zoom in, and ships just off screen could still be hit.
  • Render camera. Rules follow what's actually visible. That's honest to the cinematic rule, but zoom becomes a gameplay setting.

Leaning: a gameplay frame for now, plus a written exception for each rule that should follow what's visible.

D5

Separate world units from screen cells

Do first

Under any answer to D1, MACRO_PIXEL_SIZE has to split into two values:

  • WORLD_CELL: world units per sprite cell, a fixed part of physics
  • the screen cell: virtual px per cell, fixed under B and scaled under A

Today they're both 4.5 and nothing visible changes. Doing this before zoom work stops new code from mixing them up.

D6

Snap the camera to the cell grid?

Needs a look test

The grid is anchored to the screen. A camera moving by fractions of a cell makes every object cross cell lines at slightly different moments, so the world shimmers, and objects that move with the camera jitter by a cell. The shake already moves in whole cells. Options:

  • snap camera translation (after rotation, in screen space) to whole cells
  • allow sub-cell movement only during fast moves
  • accept the shimmer

Under A, the same question applies to the zoom level.

D7

Background, specks and parallax under zoom

  • Stars and the nebula are "too distant to move". They probably shouldn't zoom either, or should zoom only a little.
  • Specks have per-layer parallax, so zoom should be parallax-scaled too.
  • The nebula is drawn at 1.5× the play area, so zooming out past about 0.67 shows its edges. It needs a bigger image or tiling.
D8

HUD and indicators

  • The HUD stays on the steady, unzoomed view, as it does with the shake today.
  • An indicator's "off screen" should follow the render camera, but its range should be measured from the player, not the camera.
  • At extreme zoom out, indicators may be hidden.
D9

Performance budget

  • MacroPixelRenderer issues one ShapeRenderer.rect per cell.
    • Under B2 zoomed in, a 40-cell ship at 3× is about 120×120, roughly 14k rects per ship.
    • Under B zoomed out, there are fewer cells per ship but more ships, trails and effects on screen.
  • The screen grids need allocating once at their largest size, not per frame.
  • Benchmark zoom the way the effects were (3584×1982, a -PfxBench-style option).
D10

Precision

Issue #32 showed float rounding moving a point across a cell line. Zoom in multiplies that error. Keep world positions in doubles until after the camera subtraction (as directionToScreen and craftScreenPoint already do), and apply the scale to the small relative value.

Suggested first steps

All of these leave the game looking as it does now:

  1. D5: split MACRO_PIXEL_SIZE into the world cell and the screen cell.
  2. Add zoom to ViewTransform, with lengthToScreen, and make directionToScreen honest about scale. Route SpeckField through it.
  3. Remove the player-at-the-anchor shortcuts: draw the player with worldToScreen, and measure indicator range from the player.
  4. Give gameplay its own view (D4), separate from the render camera.
  5. A debug camera (offset with the mouse, zoom with the wheel, as the effects dials do) as the look-test bench for D1 (A vs B1) and D6.

Then decide D1–D3 with Jesse from the look test, and write the full design.

Open questions

  • D2: what zoom is for, the range, and who controls it.
  • D3: does a moved camera keep the player's angle, or have its own?
  • Should gameplay and cinematic cells unify? Gameplay cells are 4.5 px and cinematic cells are 9 px. Under A, a 2× zoom-in would match the cinematic cell size, which might make cutting between scenes and gameplay seamless. Is that worth anything?
  • Under B2, does Jesse want to draw art at fixed zoom steps (like sprite LODs), or accept what the import makes from the high-res source?
